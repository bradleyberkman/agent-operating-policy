# Channel: Audio (TTS briefing)

A first-class output, not a read-aloud. Some users think best away from the screen; audio is how heavy
synthesis and decision-sets get reviewed on a walk. Offer it (don't auto-generate — it spends credits) whenever
a turn produces a mental model, a decision-set, a research synthesis, or a first-principles framing.

## Structure for a walk-and-dictate workflow

Assume the user listens to a briefing of up to about ten minutes, pauses to dictate a reaction when a point
sparks one, and pastes the whole **batched** reply back after listening. So:

- **Make spark-points discrete and self-contained** — don't require holding much cross-section state in the
  head; each point should stand on its own enough to react to in isolation.
- **Summarize AND provoke.** Do not drop the summary — the user sometimes needs it. But don't end on the tidy
  conclusion either; **end on a series of numbered questions** to respond to, so the walk does real thinking.
- **Lead with the macro frame, then drill down.** Track the source doc's structure so the user's notes map to
  sections.
- Expect the reply **async and batched**, and remember **silence on a point = approval** — only flagged points
  need action.

## Mechanics

- Generate with whatever TTS skill or tool the harness provides. Still write the durable doc/artifact as the
  source of truth — audio is the review layer on top.
- Audio suits the **convergent / already-pruned** half of the work (it's sequential, unscannable, holds ~3
  threads). Do NOT push a wide divergent option-space to audio — that needs a scannable visual.
