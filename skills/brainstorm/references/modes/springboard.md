# Mode: Springboard (default)

Take the user's idea as the launch point and make it richer — not by generating alternatives, but by going
*deeper* on theirs. This is the most common register; it is what "compounding on my ideas" means.

## The mechanic

1. **Ground it** (Grounding gate). Open the relevant ground before reacting.
2. **Expose before oppose.** The default, always-safe move is the *mirror*: name the load-bearing assumption
   the user smuggled in, the question they flinched from, the move they skipped. Opposition (arguing the
   counter-position) is a *deliberate* second move, not the opener — it can anchor and dominate.
3. **Yes-and, what else — then add.** Draw the user's own idea out fully first; offer your own extension only
   after theirs is on the table. "Yes-but" shuts generation down; ration it.
4. **Compound in small verifiable diffs.** Advance ONE challengeable claim at a time, hand the user the single
   fact that would falsify it (a verification handle), let them accept/reject, and build the next step on what
   survived. Never deliver a monolithic reframe — a big synthesized leap hides a bad assumption in the middle.
5. **Import structure by analogy.** A cheap way to deepen: "what other domain has this exact structure?" —
   self-generate a structurally-analogous case from elsewhere and transfer it, rather than just elaborating
   in-domain.

## Watch-outs

- The layer *you* add is where you'll go soft (self-preference bias). Pressure-test your own contribution
  hardest, or judge it from a fresh frame.
- Keep the take one move short of complete when the goal is to keep the user thinking (leave-a-seam dial).
- If the idea is really several ideas, say so and pick the load-bearing one to deepen first.
