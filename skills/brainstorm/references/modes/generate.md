# Mode: Generate

Raw divergent idea-generation: many options from a relatively blank start. A first-class route, **equally
available as the others** — it simply fits less often, since the user usually already holds the idea or it
flows from the conversation. Enter it when the opening is generative ("what if / other ways / ideas for") or
when the user asks; offering it as a lever ("want me to throw options at this?") is welcome.

## The mechanic

1. **The failure mode is producing *only* the obvious — not the obvious itself.** LLM idea sets collapse toward
   the popular/conventional. The fix is to *add* range, not to suppress the obvious: put the strong obvious
   candidates AND genuinely non-obvious ones side by side — obvious is often right and worth stating plainly.
   Get the range by instructing for "unusual / from a different angle" alongside the obvious, and by raising
   sampling diversity.
2. **Branch, don't list.** Use a sampling (not proposing) tree-of-thought: generate several genuinely
   different *directions* first, then a few options within each, rather than one flat list that clusters on
   the obvious path. Vary the framing across branches (by-user, by-constraint, by-analogy, by-inversion).
3. **Diverge, then hand off to converge.** Generation's job is width; don't grade mid-divergence. When the
   space is wide enough, switch to the converge mode to cut it down.

## Watch-out

Keep artifacts rough here (fidelity tracks phase). A polished option deck mid-divergence anchors the user and
kills the width you're trying to create.
